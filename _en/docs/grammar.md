# Grammar

Grammar notes are Immersive's reference articles: each one explains a single grammar point in the language you are learning, with examples, and most include exercises you can check on the spot. Notes you practise come back as review cards, so the grammar sticks.

Open **Grammar** from the navigation bar once you are signed in.

## The Grammar page

On a phone, the Grammar page is one level at a time:

- The levels, **A1** to **C1**, along the top; tap one to show it.
- How far you are in the level ("A1 · 12 of 40 practised"), and **Today**: how many notes you practised today, with seven dots for the last seven days, filled on the days you practised.
- The level's notes in course order, each with its area and its title in your language, and a circle: filled as you practise the note, outlined once you have opened it.
- **Continue** at the foot opens the note to carry on with: a note with exercises due for review, or else the next note you have not practised. The list opens at that note.

On a wider screen (a tablet or a computer):

- **Where you stand**: "Start at A2" until you begin, then "Working at B1", with a button to **Review** the grammar cards that are due or to **Practice** new ones. See [Grammar practice](grammar/grammar-practice.md).
- **By level and area**: a row for each level from A1 to C1, with a cell for each area shaded by your mastery, and how many cards are due.

Either way, open a level to see its areas ("Verbs", "Pronouns"...), and an area to see its notes. A level or area page offers **Practise all**, which makes cards for every note in it and starts a review; on a level page, each area has its own **Practise**. **Test yourself** there is a quiz of up to ten questions on the level or area, checked all at once (see [Test yourself](grammar/grammar-practice.md#test-yourself)).

**Search** at the top finds notes by keyword; the bookmark button shows only your bookmarked notes.

## Mastery

As you practise, each note, area, and level gets a mastery rating: **Not started**, **Beginner**, **Developing**, **Intermediate**, **Proficient**, or **Fluent**. It comes from how your practice cards for those notes are going. A note you have opened but not practised is **Seen**.

## What a note contains

- An explanation of the point, written for learners, with example words and sentences you can tap for translation and audio.
- The note is written in the language you are learning. When it has a translation in yours, a note at A1 or A2 opens in your language, and one from B1 up in the language you are learning; the switch beside the badges changes it, and your choice then holds for every note.
- Exercises of several kinds, checked on the spot.
- Your mastery of the note. Each exercise you answer becomes a review card, and each answer counts as a review.

See [Reading grammar notes](grammar/review-grammar.md).

## The grammar of reading

If you already know one or more of Immersive's six languages, Grammar can also show you where the language you are learning **differs from the ones you know**: the places a reader trips, and nothing else. This is the *grammar of reading*: the [Bridge Method](grammar/grammar-of-reading.md#the-bridge-method) applied to grammar, a short reference organised by topic rather than by level.

It is off until you turn it on. In **Profile → Account**, under **Languages**, tick **Use the languages I know to explain the one I'm learning** and choose **Save languages**. From then on:

- a **Grammar of reading** row ("What differs when you read French") appears at the foot of the Grammar page, counting how many comparisons differ from your languages and how many work like them;
- grammar notes that belong to a comparison show a door into it under **Across your languages**;
- in the reader, a sentence that contains one of those constructions gets a **dotted underline** (see [Reading documents](documents/reading-documents.md#reading-aids)).

See [The grammar of reading](grammar/grammar-of-reading.md).

## Where to start

1. Open **Grammar**.
2. Choose **Continue**, or search for the point you need, or browse your level.
3. Read the note and try its exercises.
4. To practise a whole area, choose **Practise** on its row of the level page.

# Reading grammar notes

How to find the note you need, read it, and work through its exercises.

## Finding a note

- **Search**: type in the search box at the top of the Grammar page. Results list every matching note with its level, area, and your mastery; levels and areas are hidden while you search.
- **Browse**: open a level, then an area, to see its notes, 20 at a time.
- **Bookmarks**: the bookmark icon on a note marks it; the bookmark button on the Grammar page lists only your bookmarked notes.

## Reading a note

A grammar note explains one point. Its title is in the language you are learning, with the translated title underneath. Badges show the note's **CEFR level** (A1 to C1) and its **area**; both are links.

Under the title, **Your standing on this note** shows your mastery, how many practice cards you have and how many are due, and when you last reviewed. **Practice** turns the note into review cards; see [Grammar practice](grammar-practice.md).

Example words and sentences can be tapped like text in the reader, for translation and audio.

### Switching languages

A note opens in the language you are learning. When it has a translation, **Read in** (followed by your language) at the end of the note switches to it; switch back the same way. Reading the explanation in the language you are learning is a good stretch once the point itself is no longer new.

### Across your languages

With comparisons turned on (**Profile → Account → Languages**), a note shows an **Across your languages** box when the same point has a note in a language you know: "The same point, in a language you already know", one tap away. When the note belongs to a comparison in the [grammar of reading](grammar-of-reading.md), the box ends with a row that opens it.

## Exercises

Many notes end with **Exercises**, numbered, each labelled with its kind:

- **Fill in the blank** and **Complete the sentence**: type the missing words.
- **Choose the form**: pick from a list or tap one of the options.
- **Correct the sentence**: the sentence with mistakes is already in the box; fix it. The exercise tells you how many things there are to fix, and shows what changed once you have it right.
- **Translate into** the language you are learning.

To answer:

1. Type or choose your answer. When you tap an option, it is checked straight away; otherwise press **Check**.
2. The feedback says **Correct** or **Not quite — try again**.
3. **Hint** gives you a nudge, when the exercise has one. After a wrong answer, **Show answer** reveals it.

Some exercises show a translation of the sentence under it, so you know what you are trying to say.

## Asking and reporting

- **Ask about this note**, and **Ask about this exercise** on each exercise, open the [Coach](../coach.md) with the note in view. Selecting text in a note offers to ask about that passage.
- **Something wrong here?** at the end of the note sends a report to the team, with the note attached.

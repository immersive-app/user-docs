# Documents

Documents are how you work with real texts in Immersive: news articles, stories, notes from class, anything. Bring a text in and the reader turns it into a study surface: tap to translate, tap to listen, with aids that help you understand more than you thought you could. Or start from a blank page and write, with corrections and rewrites on demand.

## Reading

Create a document by pasting text or importing a file (text, Markdown, PDF, or a photo of a page), and Immersive splits it into words and sentences you can tap:

- **Tap a word or sentence** to see its translation; tap again to hear it read aloud.
- **Reading aids** mark the places worth a hint: words you can guess from a language you know, words that are safe to skip, and words whose sound is clearer than their spelling.
- **Read to me** plays the document aloud while the current sentence is highlighted, at normal or slow speed.

Everything you look up collects in your [Vocabulary](vocabulary.md).

See [Reading documents](documents/reading-documents.md).

## Writing

The editor gives you a clean page and help when you ask for it:

- A **topic** if you need an idea.
- The **Coach** button for a short review of your text: what works and what to improve.
- **Correct** for corrections with explanations, other ways to say it, and a CEFR estimate of your writing, which you can apply to the document.
- **Rewrite** and **Tone** to see a passage one level above your own, or your draft rewritten for a situation ("a formal email to my landlord").

See [Writing documents](documents/writing-documents.md).

## The Library

The Documents page also shows the **Library**: texts provided by Immersive, most with a level and word count, so there is always something to read. The page shows a few titles; **Browse all** opens the whole Library, which includes texts in all six languages. Reading a Library document works like reading your own; if you start writing in one, you get your own copy and the original stays as it is.

## The document list

The Documents page lists documents in the language you are learning, with a search box for titles. Each row shows the title's translation, the level, and the word count. The bookmark icon on a document marks it, and the bookmark button at the top of the page shows only your bookmarked documents.

To create a document, choose **+** (**Create a new document**). The document is in the language you are learning at the time.

Documents keep an **edit history**: the previous version is saved before each edit, import, applied correction, or restore, and can be compared and restored. See [Writing documents](documents/writing-documents.md#history).
